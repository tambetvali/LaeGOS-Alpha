## Planckton Scale Relations

In this interpretation of Laegna, the decimal series (i, o, a, e) describes increasing orders of measurable extent, while the binary series (I-, O-, A-, E-, E+) describes increasing structural complexity.

- Decimal scale increases by powers whose zero-count follows n²:
  - i = 10⁰ = 1
  - o = 10¹ = 10
  - a = 10⁴ = 10,000
  - e = 10⁹ = 1,000,000,000

- Internal binary scale:
  - I- = 1
  - O- = 2
  - A- = 4
  - E- = 16

- External binary scale:
  - I+ = 1 (or 2)
  - O+ = 4
  - A+ = 16
  - E+ = 65,536

The decimal scale may be viewed as quantity, extent, duration or volume, while the binary scale represents organizational depth or complexity.

Thus:

Planck Units
= Decimal Rank × Binary Rank

The resulting value expresses how much structured Planck-scale space, time or energy exists at that combined rank.

## Three Magnitudes

A useful extension of the Planckton system is to treat each completed rank not as an endpoint, but as the unit of the next magnitude.

Thus there are always three visible magnitudes:

- Magnitude 1 = current scale
- Magnitude 2 = next scale, where the completion of Magnitude 1 becomes the new 1
- Magnitude 3 = the scale after that

This applies independently to both the binary and decimal systems.
