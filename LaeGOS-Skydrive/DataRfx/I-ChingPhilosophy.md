## Further Interpretation

The purpose of this correspondence is not to claim historical identity between the *I Ching* and Laegna, but to use the hexagram structure as a convenient symbolic language for discussing rank, polarity, locality, interiority, exteriority, and scale.

The six-line structure can be viewed as six independent binary decisions:

$$
2^6 = 64
$$

which naturally generates the sixty-four hexagrams.

Within Laegna language, this becomes a coordinate system rather than merely an oracle.

The upper trigram describes an exterior tendency:

$$
H_{ext}
$$

while the lower trigram describes an interior tendency:

$$
H_{int}
$$

and every hexagram becomes a point inside their combined state space.

## Trigrams as Rank Directions

In traditional I Ching, a trigram represents three binary layers.

In this interpretation, each layer is associated with a rank-function pair:

| Line | Rank | Interpretation |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Upper | Y | Exponential tendency |
| Middle | X | Linear tendency |
| Lower | Z | Logarithmic tendency |

This creates a natural hierarchy:

$$
Z \rightarrow X \rightarrow Y
$$

where:

- Z describes local state.
- X describes observable behavior.
- Y describes asymptotic behavior.

The resulting structure resembles three nested scales operating simultaneously.

A point has:

- a local meaning,
- a visible meaning,
- an infinite meaning.
