**Multiplication in the linear band becomes translation in the logarithmic band.**

The two representations describe the same operation differently.

### Averaging the external representation

We can introduce a candidate external averaging function:

$$
H_{\mathrm{ext}}(u)=\frac{u+e^u}{2}
$$

This combines the logarithmic coordinate and its exponential reconstruction.

It is a proposed mathematical model for an averaged external band, not an established holographic operator.

Its purpose is to explore how linear and exponential descriptions might coexist in one representation.

The next question is whether multiplication by a golden factor creates a particularly stable relationship between the two bands.

## 5. The internal and external holographic connection

There is an established physical framework that provides a useful comparison: holographic duality, particularly AdS/CFT.

In this framework:

- A higher-dimensional gravitational spacetime, called the bulk, is related to a lower-dimensional quantum theory on its boundary.
- Information about bulk regions can be reconstructed from boundary information.
- Quantum error-correcting structures help explain how bulk information can have multiple boundary representations.

This is a genuine mathematical and physical framework, not simply a visual analogy.

A possible correspondence with Laegna is:

| Laegna interpretation | Established mathematical analogy |
|---|---|
| $H_{\mathrm{int}}$ | Internal degrees of freedom |
| $H_{\mathrm{ext}}$ | External or boundary representation |
| Linear band | Direct coordinate representation |
| Logarithmic band | Scale or rank coordinate |
| Exponential band | Reconstruction of magnitude from rank |
| Precision transfer | Information encoding and reconstruction |
| Two-band multiplication | Coupled representation of two components |

The analogy is worth investigating, but it does not establish that Laegna's particular arithmetic is already part of AdS/CFT.

The interesting research question is whether a Laegna transformation can be constructed that has mathematically comparable reconstruction properties.

## 6. Holographic multiplication as a transformation

We can now formulate three possible experiments.

### Experiment A — Golden multiplication

Compare multiplication by:

$$
2,\quad\phi,\quad1+\sqrt{5},\quad2\phi
$$

Measure how each changes the proportions between two bands.

The objective is not simply to compare their numerical magnitudes.

Instead, investigate:

- How the two bands transform.
- Whether their ratio is preserved.
- Whether precision transfers between them.
- Whether a particular multiplication creates a stable recursive structure.

The golden ratio is a particularly interesting candidate because:

$$
\phi^2=\phi+1
$$

Its multiplication naturally relates a previous scale to the next one.

### Experiment B — Fibonacci reconstruction

Use the matrix:

$$
M=\begin{pmatrix} 0&1\\ 1&1 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Repeated multiplication produces Fibonacci growth.

For example:

$$
M\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ 1 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 1\\ 2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
M\begin{pmatrix} 1\\ 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ 3 \end{pmatrix}
$$

$$
M \begin{pmatrix} 2\\ 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3\\ 5 \end{pmatrix}
$$

And subsequently:

$$
(5,8),\quad(8,13),\quad(13,21)
$$

The ratio of successive Fibonacci numbers approaches the golden ratio:

$$
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{F_{n+1}}{F_n}=\phi
$$

Investigate whether these two components can represent internal and external precision bands.

### Experiment C — Logarithmic-exponential curvature

Compare the linear representation with logarithmic and exponential projections.

For example:

$$
x=e^u
$$

A multiplication by a constant becomes:

$$
u'=u+\ln k
$$

This allows us to investigate whether the external representation can describe multiplication as a translation through numerical ranks.

A further step would be to define a curvature measure for the transformation and compare it with the original linear representation.

## 7. The open question: what does holographic separation mean?

There is an important distinction between numerical separation and holographic separation.

Numerical separation concerns the difference between two quantities.

Holographic separation would concern the distinguishability of two components across different representations.

For example, two bands might have different numerical values but become more clearly distinguishable when projected into logarithmic coordinates.

Alternatively, two numerically different quantities might become equivalent under an external normalization.

To make this precise, we need to define a separation measure.

One possible starting point is:

$$
D(B_1,B_2)=
\left|
\ln\frac{B_1}{B_2}
\right|
$$

For the golden partition:

$$
D(B_1,B_2)=\ln\phi
$$

This measures their multiplicative separation in logarithmic coordinates.

It is not yet a measure of holographic information or precision conservation, but it provides an exact quantity that can be compared across different band transformations.

We could also investigate how the separation changes after repeated transformations:

$$
\mathbf{b}_{n+1}=T\mathbf{b}_n
$$

Where $T$ is a defined Laegna transformation.

The central question becomes whether there is an invariant:

$$
I(T\mathbf{b})=I(\mathbf{b})
$$

Such an invariant could represent preserved information, a rank relation, or another mathematically defined property.

Finding it would be a substantial step toward formalizing HOp.

## 8. The deeper possibility

The golden ratio is interesting because it combines three properties:

1. It relates a whole to a recursively related part.
2. It emerges naturally from a two-component recurrence.
3. It is the limiting ratio of Fibonacci growth.

These properties give us a possible bridge between arithmetic, recursion, and geometry.

In Laegna, we can ask whether the same structure can be used to describe:

- Internal and external numerical representations.
- Linear and logarithmic-exponential bands.
- Precision transfer between ranks.
- Recursive multiplication.
- Observer and observed curvature.

The established mathematics provides a starting point, but the Laegna interpretation requires its own formal definitions.

Perhaps the most useful insight is this:

**A number can have a linear magnitude while simultaneously possessing an external representation in which multiplication becomes translation and recursive growth becomes a relationship between bands.**

The golden ratio gives us an especially elegant candidate for studying that relationship.

The next step is not to assume that the golden ratio solves holographic arithmetic, but to discover exactly which properties of its recursive structure can be preserved when the number is rotated between Laegna representations.

That is where HOp becomes a mathematical research programme rather than merely a numerical analogy.
