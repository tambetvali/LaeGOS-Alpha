# A History of Number Systems
### From counting stones to zero, infinity, and the digital world

Numbers are among humanity's oldest inventions. Before mathematics became a science, counting was a practical activity: measuring food, dividing land, remembering livestock, tracking seasons, and recording debts.

Yet numbers gradually became something more. Civilizations associated them with gods, cosmic order, geometry, time, life, death, and the structure of the universe.

The history of number systems is therefore also a history of how humans learned to represent reality.

One important distinction runs throughout this story:

- A **number** is an abstract mathematical quantity.
- A **numeral** is a written symbol representing a number.
- A **number system** is a set of symbols and rules for representing numbers.

The same number can look completely different in different civilizations.

---

## 1. Tally marks — The first numerical language

**Approximate history:** Prehistoric, tens of thousands of years ago.

Before writing existed, people could count without having numerical words or a formal numeral system. A notch in a bone, a mark on wood, or a stone placed in a pile could represent one additional object.

One famous example is the Ishango bone, discovered in Central Africa, dating to approximately 20,000 years ago. Its groups of notches have attracted mathematical interpretations, although their exact purpose remains debated.

### The numerical alphabet

| Number | Symbol | Meaning |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | \| | One object |
| 2 | \|\| | Two objects |
| 3 | \|\|\| | Three objects |
| 4 | \|\|\|\| | Four objects |
| 5 | \|\|\|\|/ | A grouped quantity |
| 10 | \|\|\|\|/ \|\|\|\|/ | Two groups of five |

### Writing rules

The simplest system is additive:

- Each mark represents one unit.
- More marks represent larger quantities.
- Groups make counting easier.
- There is no positional value.
- There is no mathematical zero.

### Real associations

Tally systems were useful for:
- Livestock.
- Trading.
- Hunting records.
- Lunar observations.
- Days and seasons.
- Personal debts.

### Mythic associations

Counting was closely connected to the cycles of nature. Five fingers, four directions, seven visible celestial wanderers, and twelve lunar or seasonal divisions became recurring patterns in human symbolic traditions.

These associations were not universal, but they helped numbers acquire cultural significance.

**Mathematical importance:** Counting marks introduced the fundamental relationship between quantity and representation.

---

## 2. Sumerian numerals — The birth of recorded mathematics

**Approximate history:** 3400–3000 BCE, Mesopotamia.

The Sumerians developed one of the earliest known systems of numerical writing. Their civilization needed accounting for agriculture, temples, trade, taxation, and administration.

Clay tokens and accounting practices gradually developed into written numerical signs on clay tablets.

Early Mesopotamian systems were not identical to the later Babylonian system. They used several counting conventions, including decimal and sexagesimal groupings.

### Numerical alphabet

The following are simplified Unicode approximations of the cuneiform numeral signs:

| Number | Approximate symbol | Description |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | `𒁹` | Single unit wedge |
| 2 | `𒁹𒁹` | Two units |
| 3 | `𒁹𒁹𒁹` | Three units |
| 4 | `𒁹𒁹𒁹𒁹` | Four units |
| 5 | `𒁹 × 5` | Five units |
| 10 | `𒌋` | Ten-unit sign |
| 20 | `𒌋𒌋` | Two tens |

*The exact signs and their values depend on the period and numerical context. These are illustrative rather than a complete historical sign chart.*

### Writing rules

- Numbers were recorded using combinations of wedge-shaped signs.
- Grouping simplified large quantities.
- Different metrological systems existed for different commodities.
- The notation was primarily additive in its early forms.
- There was no universal positional decimal notation.

### Real associations

Sumerian numerical writing was closely related to the emergence of organized civilization.

It supported:
- Agricultural administration.
- Temple inventories.
- Land measurement.
- Construction.
- Trade.
- Early astronomical records.

### Mythic associations

Mesopotamian civilization associated cosmic order with divine administration.

The gods governed different aspects of existence, while temples represented connections between the human world and the heavens.

Numbers became essential to understanding calendars, celestial cycles, and the organization of the universe.

**Mathematical importance:** Numbers became a permanent, transferable form of information.

## 3. Babylonian numerals — The civilization of 60

**Approximate history:** From approximately 2000 BCE.

The Babylonians inherited earlier Mesopotamian mathematics and developed a remarkably powerful positional system.

Their most important innovation was the use of positional notation based on 60.

We still encounter their mathematical legacy every day:

- 60 seconds in a minute.
- 60 minutes in an hour.
- 360 degrees in a circle.

### Numerical alphabet

The Babylonian system used two fundamental wedge signs.

| Value | Cuneiform approximation | Meaning |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | `𒁹` | One |
| 2 | `𒁹𒁹` | Two |
| 3 | `𒁹𒁹𒁹` | Three |
| 4 | `𒁹𒁹𒁹𒁹` | Four |
| 5 | `𒁹 × 5` | Five |
| 10 | `𒌋` | Ten |
| 20 | `𒌋𒌋` | Twenty |
| 30 | `𒌋 × 3` | Thirty |
| 40 | `𒌋 × 4` | Forty |
| 50 | `𒌋 × 5` | Fifty |

### Writing rules

Babylonian notation was positional.

Each position represented a power of 60:

| Position | Mathematical value |
|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 60 |
| 3 | 3,600 |
| 4 | 216,000 |
| 5 | 12,960,000 |

For example:

`1, 20` in sexagesimal means:

$$
1 \times 60 + 20 = 80
$$

And:

`2, 15` means:

$$
2 \times 60 + 15 = 135
$$

Originally, an empty position was often indicated by spacing or context. A placeholder sign appeared in later Babylonian astronomical notation, but it was not a fully developed zero equivalent to our modern zero.

### Real associations

The base-60 system was particularly useful for:
- Astronomy.
- Angular measurements.
- Calendars.
- Geometry.
- Dividing quantities into convenient fractions.

Sixty has many divisors:

$$
60=2^2 \times 3 \times 5
$$

This makes it convenient for practical subdivisions.

### Mythic associations

Babylonian astronomy connected numerical order with celestial order.

The heavens were interpreted through cycles, planetary movements, divine powers, and mathematical relationships.

The number 360 became particularly important because it approximates the number of days in an ancient idealized year and provides a convenient division of the circle.

**Mathematical importance:** Positional notation allowed a small collection of symbols to represent extremely large numbers.

## 4. Egyptian numerals — Numbers as hieroglyphs

**Approximate history:** From approximately 3000 BCE.

Ancient Egyptian numerical notation was decimal, but not positional.

The Egyptians used distinct hieroglyphs for successive powers of ten. Larger numbers were constructed by repeating the appropriate symbols.

### Numerical alphabet

| Number | Symbol | Meaning |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | `𓏺` | Single stroke |
| 10 | `𓎆` | Heel bone or cattle hobble |
| 100 | `𓍢` | Coil of rope |
| 1,000 | `𓆼` | Lotus flower |
| 10,000 | `𓂭` | Pointing finger |
| 100,000 | `𓆐` | Tadpole or frog |
| 1,000,000 | `𓁨` | Heh figure, associated with immense quantity or eternity |

### Writing rules

Egyptian numbers were additive.

For example:

$$
2,345 = 2(1000)+3(100)+4(10)+5(1)
$$

The number was written using:
- Two thousand symbols.
- Three hundred symbols.
- Four ten symbols.
- Five unit symbols.

There was no place-value system in the modern sense.

### Real associations

Egyptian mathematics was used in:
- Pyramid construction.
- Land surveying.
- Taxation.
- Grain storage.
- Calendar calculations.
- Astronomy.
- Religious architecture.

### Mythic associations

Egyptian numerical symbolism was strongly connected to divine cosmology.

| Number | Symbolic association |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Unity and the primordial source |
| 2 | Duality and complementary forces |
| 3 | Completeness and divine triads |
| 4 | Four directions and spatial order |
| 7 | Sacred completeness |
| 9 | The Ennead, a group of nine deities |
| 12 | Celestial and temporal divisions |
| 42 | The forty-two divine assessors of judgment |
| 1,000,000 | Immensity and eternity |

The million-sign Heh is especially interesting: an image of a figure associated with innumerability became a numerical sign for one million.

**Mathematical importance:** Egypt demonstrated how a decimal system could function without positional notation.

## 5. Greek numerals — Numbers become letters

**Approximate history:** From approximately the 6th century BCE.

Ancient Greece developed more than one numerical notation.

Two particularly important systems were the Attic (or acrophonic) system and the later Ionic (alphabetic) system.

The alphabetic system is especially interesting because it turned the ordinary alphabet into a numerical alphabet.

### 5.1 Attic numerals

The Attic system used symbols associated with the initial letters of numerical words.

| Number | Symbol | Meaning |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | `Ι` | One |
| 5 | `Π` | Five |
| 10 | `Δ` | Ten |
| 100 | `Η` | Hundred |
| 1,000 | `Χ` | Thousand |
| 10,000 | `Μ` | Ten thousand |

### Writing rules

Numbers were assembled additively from the corresponding symbols.

For example, 16 could be written as:

`Δ Π Ι`

Representing:

$$
10+5+1=16
$$

### Writing rules

The Ionic system used three groups:

A small mark called the keraia distinguished numerical usage from ordinary letters.

For example:

### Real associations

Greek mathematics made extraordinary advances in:
- Geometry.
- Number theory.
- Proportions.
- Astronomy.
- Logical proof.
- Mathematical philosophy.

Pythagorean mathematics associated numerical ratios with musical harmony and geometric relationships.

```text
    • • • •
```

Its total is:

$$
1+2+3+4=10
$$

The number ten represented an ideal completeness in Pythagorean thought.

**Mathematical importance:** Greek culture connected numerical structures with abstract reasoning, geometry, and philosophy.

## 6. Roman numerals — The language of empire

**Approximate history:** Developed over centuries before and during the Roman Republic and Empire.

Roman numerals are among the most recognizable historical numeral systems.

They remain visible on clocks, monuments, book chapters, buildings, and formal documents.

### Numerical alphabet

| Number | Symbol | Meaning |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | I | One |
| 5 | V | Five |
| 10 | X | Ten |
| 50 | L | Fifty |
| 100 | C | Hundred |
| 500 | D | Five hundred |
| 1,000 | M | Thousand |

### Writing rules

Roman numerals use additive and subtractive principles.

- Repeated symbols are generally added.
- A smaller symbol before a larger symbol is subtracted.
- A smaller symbol after a larger symbol is added.
- Repetition is limited by conventional rules.

Examples:

| Numeral | Calculation | Value |
|:---:|---|---:|
| II | 1+1 | 2 |
| III | 1+1+1 | 3 |
| IV | 5−1 | 4 |
| VI | 5+1 | 6 |
| IX | 10−1 | 9 |
| XIV | 10+5−1 | 14 |
| XIX | 10+10−1 | 19 |
| XL | 50−10 | 40 |
| XC | 100−10 | 90 |
| CD | 500−100 | 400 |
| CM | 1,000−100 | 900 |
| MMXXVI | 2,000+20+6 | 2,026 |

### Real associations

Roman numerals were used for inscriptions, military records, administration, monetary accounting, architecture, and chronology.

Complex calculations were generally performed with counting boards and other calculating devices rather than directly with written Roman numerals.

### Mythic associations

Roman culture inherited much of its numerical philosophy from Greece.

Its numerals became associated with imperial authority, military organization, civic order, and monumental permanence.

**Mathematical importance:** Roman numerals were excellent for recording and displaying quantities, but inefficient for advanced arithmetic.

## 7. Chinese numerals — Numbers as organized strokes

**Approximate history:** At least the second millennium BCE.

Chinese numerical writing developed through several historical stages, including oracle-bone inscriptions, bronze inscriptions, counting rods, and traditional written numerals.

The system combines decimal grouping with recognizable numerical characters.

### Numerical alphabet

| Number | Traditional form | Modern simplified form | Description |
|---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| 0 | 〇 | 〇 | Empty or zero |
| 1 | 一 | 一 | One horizontal stroke |

### Counting rods

An especially important Chinese innovation was the use of counting rods.

Numbers were represented by arranging rods in decimal positions. Alternating vertical and horizontal arrangements distinguished adjacent positions.

This was a genuine positional calculation method.

It supported sophisticated mathematical procedures long before modern written decimal notation became widespread.

## 8. Hebrew numerals — The alphabet of sacred numbers

**Approximate history:** Developed from ancient Hebrew alphabetic notation, with historical roots in the first millennium BCE.

Hebrew numerals assign numerical values to letters. The system is related to the wider family of alphabetic numerical notations.

### Numerical alphabet

| Number | Hebrew | Name |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 1 | א | Aleph |
| 2 | ב | Bet |
| 3 | ג | Gimel |
| 4 | ד | Dalet |
| 5 | ה | He |
| 6 | ו | Vav |
| 7 | ז | Zayin |
| 8 | ח | Het |
| 9 | ט | Tet |
| 10 | י | Yod |
| 20 | כ | Kaf |
| 30 | ל | Lamed |
| 40 | מ | Mem |
| 50 | נ | Nun |
| 60 | ס | Samekh |
| 70 | ע | Ayin |
| 80 | פ | Pe |
| 90 | צ | Tsadi |
| 100 | ק | Qof |
| 200 | ר | Resh |
| 300 | ש | Shin |
| 400 | ת | Tav |

Additional final letter forms can represent 500–900 in some traditions.

### Writing rules

- Letters represent numerical values.
- Numbers are generally assembled additively.
- Special punctuation distinguishes numbers from words.
- The standard system does not use a positional zero.

For example:

`ט״ו` = 15.

`כ״ג` = 23.

Certain combinations are avoided in religious contexts because they resemble divine names.

### Mythic and religious associations

Hebrew numerical interpretation is particularly associated with Gematria.

Gematria assigns numerical values to words and explores relationships between words with equal totals.

For example:

$$
יהוה = 10+5+6+5=26
$$

This numerical value has religious and interpretive significance within Jewish mystical traditions.

The practice should be understood as a symbolic interpretive tradition, not as evidence that words with equal numerical values necessarily share a historical or scientific relationship.

**Mathematical importance:** Alphabetic notation shows how an existing writing system can be extended to represent numbers.

## 9. Maya numerals — The independent discovery of zero

**Approximate history:** Developed in Mesoamerica before the Common Era; securely attested in the Maya tradition by the late Preclassic period.

The Maya created one of the most fascinating numerical systems in human history.

They used a positional system based primarily on twenty, a shell-shaped zero, dots representing units, and bars representing groups of five.

### Numerical alphabet

| Number | Maya-style symbol | Meaning |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 0 | Shell | Zero |
| 1 | `•` | One |
| 2 | `••` | Two |
| 3 | `•••` | Three |
| 4 | `••••` | Four |
| 5 | `━` | Five |
| 6 | `━ •` | Six |
| 7 | `━ ••` | Seven |
| 8 | `━ •••` | Eight |
| 9 | `━ ••••` | Nine |
| 10 | `━━` | Ten |
| 15 | `━━━` | Fifteen |
| 19 | `━━━━ ••••` | Nineteen |

*The shell and dot/bar forms are simplified representations. Historical glyph forms varied.*

### Writing rules

The Maya used a vigesimal system:

$$
20^0=1
$$

$$
20^1=20
$$

$$
20^2=400
$$

$$
20^3=8000
$$

Numbers were generally written vertically, with the lowest position at the bottom.

For example, the decimal number 43:

    ••       2 × 20
    ━━━      3 × 1

$$
2(20)+3=43
$$

### The calendar exception

Maya calendar notation used a modified positional progression:

$$
1,\ 20,\ 360,\ 7200,\ldots
$$

The third position was 18 × 20 rather than 20 × 20.

This produced a convenient relationship with the 360-day calendar year.

### Real associations

The system supported astronomical observations, calendar calculations, historical chronology, ritual scheduling, and long-term timekeeping.

### Mythic associations

Maya cosmology connected numbers with creation, cycles of time, celestial movements, and sacred calendars.

The Long Count calendar represented time through successive numerical periods.

Zero was especially significant because it allowed the recording of an empty position within a numerical sequence.

**Mathematical importance:** The Maya independently developed a sophisticated positional system with zero, demonstrating that the concept was not exclusive to Eurasian mathematics.

## 10. Inca quipu — Numbers without a conventional alphabet

**Approximate history:** Especially developed during the Inca Empire, 15th–16th centuries CE, with earlier Andean precedents.

The Inca used quipu, a system of cords and knots, for recording information.

Unlike the numeral systems discussed above, quipu was not primarily a written alphabet of numerical glyphs.

It was a material information system.

### Numerical representation

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Main cord | Organizes the record |
| Pendant cord | Holds numerical information |
| Knot position | Represents decimal position |
| Single knot | Represents groups of units |
| Long knot | Represents groups of units |
| Figure-eight knot | Represents one |
| No knot in a position | Represents an empty place |

### Writing rules

Quipu used decimal positional organization.

A cord could contain positions for units, tens, hundreds, thousands, and higher powers of ten.

The arrangement and types of knots encoded quantities.

### Real associations

Quipu were used for census information, tribute and taxation, agricultural production, storage inventories, and administrative accounting.

Some quipu may also have encoded non-numerical information, although the extent and exact interpretation remain subjects of research.

### Mythic associations

Andean cosmology associated order with relationships between humans, ancestors, mountains, agriculture, and the natural world.

Quipu represented an organized memory of society: a way to preserve relationships and obligations across distance and generations.

**Mathematical importance:** A numerical system does not have to be written on a flat surface. Information can be encoded through physical structure.

## 11. Indian numerals — The revolution of decimal place value

**Approximate history:** Brahmi numerals are attested from around the 3rd century BCE; the fully developed decimal place-value system emerged over subsequent centuries.

The Indian mathematical tradition contributed the most influential numerical notation in use today.

The modern digits developed through historical changes in Indian numeral forms, particularly through the Brahmi and later Nagari traditions.

The essential innovation was not simply nine symbols.

It was the combination of ten numerical digits, decimal positional notation, and a symbol for zero.

### Brahmi numerals

Early Brahmi numerals had distinct symbols for numbers and multiples of ten.

Their forms changed substantially over time.

They were not yet the modern nine-digit positional system.

### Modern Indian decimal alphabet

| Number | Devanagari | Latin equivalent |
|---:|:---:|:---:|
| 0 | ० | 0 |
| 1 | १ | 1 |
| 2 | २ | 2 |
| 3 | ३ | 3 |
| 4 | ४ | 4 |
| 5 | ५ | 5 |
| 6 | ६ | 6 |
| 7 | ७ | 7 |
| 8 | ८ | 8 |
| 9 | ९ | 9 |

### Writing rules

Each position represents a power of ten.

$$
3456=3(1000)+4(100)+5(10)+6
$$

Zero occupies an empty position.

For example:

$$
305=3(100)+0(10)+5
$$

Without zero, the notation would lose the information that the tens position is empty.

### The mathematical development of zero

The Sanskrit word *śūnya* (शून्य) means empty or void.

Indian mathematicians developed increasingly sophisticated ways to treat zero as a mathematical object.

Brahmagupta, in the 7th century CE, gave systematic rules for arithmetic involving zero and negative numbers.

His treatment was historically important, although division by zero was not resolved correctly.

### Mythic and philosophical associations

The concept of śūnya is particularly interesting because emptiness already had deep philosophical meanings in Indian traditions.

However, philosophical emptiness and mathematical zero are not identical concepts.

The Buddhist concept of śūnyatā, for example, concerns the absence of independent inherent existence, rather than simply the numerical value zero.

Nevertheless, the linguistic and cultural relationship makes this history fascinating.

**Mathematical importance:** Positional decimal notation with zero made arithmetic dramatically more efficient and helped enable modern mathematics.

## 12. Arabic and Islamic mathematical notation — The transmission and expansion

**Approximate history:** From the 8th century CE onward.

The Indian numerical system spread through the Islamic world, where scholars adopted, developed, and transmitted its mathematical methods.

The word *algorithm* ultimately comes from the Latinized name of the Persian mathematician al-Khwarizmi.

The word *algebra* derives from the Arabic *al-jabr*, associated with the title of his influential mathematical work.

### Eastern Arabic numeral alphabet

| Number | Arabic-Indic | Western form |
|---:|:---:|:---:|
| 0 | ٠ | 0 |
| 1 | ١ | 1 |
| 2 | ٢ | 2 |
| 3 | ٣ | 3 |
| 4 | ٤ | 4 |
| 5 | ٥ | 5 |
| 6 | ٦ | 6 |
| 7 | ٧ | 7 |
| 8 | ٨ | 8 |
| 9 | ٩ | 9 |

### Writing rules

The decimal positional principle is the same as in modern Western numerals.

However, Arabic writing generally proceeds from right to left, and numerical notation has its own directional conventions.

There are also different regional numeral forms, including Eastern Arabic and Western Arabic varieties.

### Real associations

Islamic mathematics made important contributions to algebra, geometry, trigonometry, astronomy, arithmetic, numerical algorithms, and scientific translation.

Scholars preserved, translated, criticized, and extended Greek, Indian, Persian, and other mathematical traditions.

### Mythic and cultural associations

Numbers were important in Islamic architecture, astronomy, calendars, and geometric ornamentation.

Islamic geometric art frequently used repeated numerical and spatial relationships to produce complex patterns.

The emphasis was often on mathematical order, symmetry, and the unity of creation.

**Mathematical importance:** The Islamic scientific world became a major bridge between Indian mathematical notation and Europe.

## 13. Fibonacci and the European adoption of Hindu-Arabic numerals

**Approximate history:** 10th–16th centuries CE.

The decimal positional system gradually entered Europe through Mediterranean scientific and commercial contacts.

Leonardo of Pisa, known as Fibonacci, helped popularize the system through his book *Liber Abaci*, published in 1202.

The European transition was gradual. Roman numerals remained widely used for centuries.

### Numerical alphabet

| Number | Western digit | Historical description |
|---:|:---:|---|
| 0 | 0 | Zero |
| 1 | 1 | One |
| 2 | 2 | Two |
| 3 | 3 | Three |
| 4 | 4 | Four |
| 5 | 5 | Five |
| 6 | 6 | Six |
| 7 | 7 | Seven |
| 8 | 8 | Eight |
| 9 | 9 | Nine |

### Writing rules

The modern decimal system uses positional notation:

$$
a_n10^n+\cdots+a_2 10^2+a_1 10+a_0
$$

Every digit has a value determined by its position.

For example:

$$
2026=2(1000)+0(100)+2(10)+6
$$

The system also supports decimal fractions.

### Real associations

The system transformed commerce, accounting, banking, engineering, scientific calculation, navigation, and astronomy.

### Cultural associations

The number zero became increasingly accepted as a mathematical symbol, although its philosophical interpretation varied.

The idea that nothing could occupy a meaningful position was revolutionary in practical arithmetic.

**Mathematical importance:** This is the direct ancestor of the standard decimal notation used throughout the modern world.

## 14. Binary numerals — The language of two states

**Historical development:** Binary arithmetic has ancient conceptual precedents, but modern systematic binary arithmetic was developed by Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz in the 17th–18th centuries.

Binary is a positional number system with base two.

It uses only two digits.

| 1 | 1 | One unit |
| 2 | 10 | One group of two |
| 3 | 11 | Two plus one |
| 4 | 100 | One group of four |
| 5 | 101 | Four plus one |
| 6 | 110 | Four plus two |
| 7 | 111 | Four plus two plus one |
| 8 | 1000 | One group of eight |
| 9 | 1001 | Eight plus one |
| 10 | 1010 | Eight plus two |
| 15 | 1111 | Eight plus four plus two plus one |
| 16 | 10000 | One group of sixteen |

### Writing rules

Each position represents a power of two.

$$
1011_2=1(8)+0(4)+1(2)+1(1)
$$

$$
1011_2=11_{10}
$$

### Real associations

Binary is fundamental to digital computers, logic circuits, data storage, cryptography, communication, machine learning, and digital signal processing.

A computer does not need to understand human decimal notation internally. It can operate on electrical states represented through binary abstractions.

### Mythic and philosophical associations

Binary notation has interesting parallels with ancient dualistic philosophies.

Examples include Yin and Yang, light and darkness, active and passive, presence and absence, and true and false.

Leibniz himself was interested in the I Ching's broken and unbroken lines and recognized a structural similarity with binary notation.

However, the historical origins of binary arithmetic and the I Ching are distinct.

**Mathematical importance:** A system with only two symbols can represent every integer and support arbitrarily complex computation.

## 16. Other important numerical systems

Not every mathematical tradition uses a positional alphabet with a zero.

Several other systems are worth recognizing.

### 16.1 Japanese and Chinese rod numerals

Counting rods were used in East Asian mathematics for practical calculations.

They represented decimal positions through arrangements of vertical and horizontal rods.

The method was particularly useful for solving systems of equations and performing calculations.

### 16.2 Suzhou numerals

Traditional Chinese commercial notation used a special system of numerals called Suzhou numerals.

| Number | Symbol |
|---:|:---:|
| 1 | 〡 |
| 2 | 〢 |
| 3 | 〣 |
| 4 | 〤 |
| 5 | 〥 |
| 6 | 〦 |
| 7 | 〧 |
| 8 | 〨 |
| 9 | 〩 |
| 10 | 十 |

Their orientation and arrangement depended on the position of the digit.

They were historically useful in commerce and accounting.

### 16.3 Quinary — Base five

Base five is a natural system for counting using one hand.

| Decimal | Base five |
|---:|:---:|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 3 |
| 4 | 4 |
| 5 | 10 |
| 6 | 11 |
| 7 | 12 |
| 8 | 13 |
| 9 | 14 |
| 10 | 20 |
| 25 | 100 |

Its structure follows:

$$
5^0,\ 5^1,\ 5^2,\ 5^3,\ldots
$$

### 16.4 Duodecimal — Base twelve

Base twelve is historically interesting because twelve has many divisors.

Its factors are:

$$
1,2,3,4,6,12
$$

This makes it useful for division into equal parts.

Historical counting traditions sometimes used finger-joint counting, allowing twelve units to be counted on one hand.

### 16.5 Sexagesimal — Base sixty

The Babylonian system remains relevant because 60 has many divisors.

Its modern descendants include time measurement, angular measurement, and astronomical coordinates.

### 16.6 Bijective numeration

Some systems do not use a zero digit.

In bijective base-k notation, the available digits represent 1 through k rather than 0 through k−1.

For example, spreadsheet column labels behave like a bijective base-26 system:

| Number | Label |
|---:|:---:|
| 1 | A |
| 2 | B |
| 3 | C |
| 25 | Y |
| 26 | Z |
| 27 | AA |
| 28 | AB |
| 52 | AZ |
| 53 | BA |

This is a reminder that positional notation does not necessarily require a zero symbol, although zero makes ordinary positional arithmetic considerably more convenient.

# 19. What actually changed throughout history?

There are several major conceptual revolutions.

### First revolution: Quantity becomes representation

A mark can represent something that is no longer physically present.

This is the foundation of abstract numerical thought.

### Second revolution: Grouping

Instead of recording one hundred individual objects, one can record groups of ten or one hundred.

This creates more efficient numerical notation.

### Third revolution: Position

The position of a symbol determines its value.

This makes it possible to represent arbitrarily large quantities with relatively few symbols.

### Fourth revolution: Zero

Zero makes an empty position explicit.

It also becomes an independent mathematical object, allowing more systematic treatment of negative numbers, algebra, and arithmetic.

### Fifth revolution: Multiple bases

A number does not belong to a particular numeral system.

The same quantity can be represented in decimal, binary, octal, hexadecimal, sexagesimal, or any other base.

For example:

$$
64_{10}=1000000_2=100_8=40_{16}
$$

### Sixth revolution: Computation

Numerical representation becomes a language for machines.

Numbers can now represent quantities, text, images, sound, physical measurements, probabilities, and neural-network parameters.

The modern computer is essentially a machine for transforming structured numerical representations.

# 20. A unified mathematical perspective

The history of numeral systems reveals that the same mathematical reality can be represented through different symbolic architectures.

Consider the number 20:

| System | Representation | Structural principle |
|---|---|---|
| Tally | Twenty individual marks | Repeated marks |
| Roman | `XX` | Additive symbols |
| Greek Ionic | `Κʹ` | Alphabetic value |
| Hebrew | `כ` | Alphabetic value |
| Chinese | `二十` | Multiplicative grouping |
| Maya | One dot in the second position | Base-20 positional notation |
| Decimal | `20` | Base-10 positional notation |
| Binary | `10100` | Base-2 positional notation |
| Octal | `24` | Base-8 positional notation |
| Hexadecimal | `14` | Base-16 positional notation |

All these representations refer to the same quantity.

The difference is not in the number itself, but in the rules used to encode it.

For any positional base $b$, a number can be represented as:

$$
N=\sum_{i=0}^{n}d_i b^i
$$

where:

$$
0\leq d_i < b
$$

The base determines the available digits and the value assigned to each position.

This is the mathematical principle behind almost every modern positional numeral system.

# 21. Conclusion — From the marks of existence to the language of mathematics

Humanity did not invent numbers in one moment.

We gradually developed ways to preserve quantities, organize trade, understand celestial movements, measure land, describe geometry, and investigate the structure of reality.

Different civilizations arrived at different solutions.

- Mesopotamia developed powerful sexagesimal mathematics.
- Egypt developed a sophisticated additive decimal system.
- Greece connected numbers with philosophical and geometric reasoning.
- Rome created a durable notation for administration and monuments.
- China developed powerful calculation traditions and counting-rod arithmetic.
- The Maya independently developed positional notation with zero.
- India developed the decimal positional system that became the foundation of modern arithmetic.
- Islamic mathematicians transmitted and expanded these methods.
- Europe gradually adopted them.
- Modern computing developed binary and other positional representations.

The deeper lesson is that numbers are independent of their written forms.

A number can be expressed as a word, a letter, a wedge, a knot, a dot, a line, a geometric arrangement, or an electrical state.

The symbols change. The mathematical relationships remain.

And perhaps this is the most interesting historical transition of all:

**Numbers began as records of things. They became representations of abstract quantities. Eventually, they became a universal language for describing structures, relationships, and transformations.**
