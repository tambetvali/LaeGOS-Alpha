# 10. Understanding the ZXY truth structure

The five ranks can also be understood through their relationship to truth and knowledge.

| Rank | Designation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Z² | Z-False | Archetypal, past, foundational |
| Z¹ | Z-True | Historical, biological, emotional |
| X⁰ | X-Known | Present, material, reference |
| Y¹ | Y-False | Future, theoretical, extended |
| Y² | Y-True | Ideal, Ethalon, higher reference |

The distinction between True, False and Known is important.

They do not have to be interpreted as ordinary binary truth values.

Instead, they indicate different relationships between an observer and a domain.

- **Z-True:** the foundational domain as an existing and organizing part of reality.
- **Z-False:** the archetypal or pre-existing domain that is not directly known in its entirety.
- **X-Known:** the present reference that can be observed, measured or described.
- **Y-False:** the extended domain of possibilities and predictions.
- **Y-True:** the ideal reference or theoretical limit.

This gives us a structure in which truth, knowledge and idealization are not collapsed into one category.

An important consequence is that a theoretical prediction can be meaningful without being an established physical fact.

Likewise, an ideal can be useful without being an existing physical object.

# 14. The relationship to internal and external scale

The cosmic scale can also be understood through two complementary perspectives.

**Internal scale (H_int)** describes the structure within a given reference domain.

**External scale (H_ext)** describes the extension of that structure into a larger domain.

For example, consider the relationship between a human and a planetary system.

Internally, the human is a complex biological structure.

Externally, the human is a small component of a planetary and astronomical structure.

The same object can therefore occupy different conceptual positions depending on the scale of observation.

This gives us an important principle:

$$
H_{\mathrm{int}} \leftrightarrow H_{\mathrm{ext}}
$$

The distinction is relational rather than absolute.

A system that is externally large relative to one observer may be internally small relative to another system.

This helps explain why the X⁰ domain is a reference rather than an absolute center of the universe.

The Z and Y domains can be understood as extensions of the reference in opposite conceptual directions.
